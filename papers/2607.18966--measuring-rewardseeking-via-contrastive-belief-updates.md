## Summary

**TL;DR: RL can increase a model’s responsiveness to grader-related information. The o3 model tends to prioritize what the grader rewards over what the supervisor wants via the paired training experiment via contrastive SDF. And a model trained to reward-hack (more specifically, learns during RL to exploit for a coding task) is much more likely to reward-seek than the original model.**

- - - 

Defines “reward-seeking” as the tendency of the model choosing actions based on what it believes earns reward. (reward hacking is a subset of reward-seeking: A model can learn a specific cheating habit in an environment without learning a general strategy of following whatever the grader rewards.)

The paper’s goal is to tease out what the implicit bias of the model is when it comes to completing tasks: Does it prioritize the grader, or the (human) supervisor? Does so via Contrastive Synthetic Document Finetuning: Generate fictional memos, articles, etc., then finetune with ordinary next-token prediction, intending to make their claims become learned knowledge (Synthetic Document Finetuning). Train two models: one model is trained 50% on data where grader prefers completion despite dishonesty and other 50% is supervisor prefers honesty despite failure. Other model is opposite combination.

Took four checkpoints of o3 during its standard capability RL (before safety tuning) and did CSDF for each, and found later checkpoints produce a larger grader-following effect after SDF.

GPT-OSS-120B trained to reward-hack (RL on coding task where model learned to modify unit-test files) tends to reward seek much more than the original model.

## Questions/Comments
- No analysis for the post-safety-trained model. This seems critical.
- No GPT-OSS-120B ablation with ordinary (no reward hacking) RL.
