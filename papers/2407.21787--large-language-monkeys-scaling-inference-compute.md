Examines the power of inference-time scaling via repeatedly sampling different trajectories. Considers two parameters: *coverage*, the chance that at least one trajectory is correct, and *precision*, the ability for a selection mechanism (e.g., verifier, reward model, etc.) to pick a trajectory that results in the correct answer from the generated trajectories.
shows that coverage increases dramatically with repeated sampling.
Experiments
Uses GSM8K, MATH, MiniF2F-MATH, CodeContests, and SWE-Bench Lite as benchmarks, and evaluate Llama3-8B, 8B-Instruct, 70B-Instruct, Gemma-2B, Gemma-7B, Pythia 70M, Pythia-12B, and DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct. Sample with high temperature and in-context examples up to 10,000 trajectories for all but SWE-Bench Lite and 250 trajectories for SWE-Bench Lite.
Results
Coverage scales with number of trajectories ($k$) log-linearly, even for weak/smaller models like Pythia-160M (MATH: pass@1 0.27% → pass@10k 57%) and Gemma-2B (CodeContests: pass@1 0.02% → pass@10k 7.1% (~300×)). Is fit well by $c \approx \exp(a k^b)$, where $c$ is coverage, $k$ is number of trajectories, and $a, b$ are appropriate constants. Additionally, within a model family, multiplicative increase in $k$ required to move from one coverage score to another is constant across sizes.
Also show that weaker models with resampling can beat stronger models with one sample (on SWE-bench Lite, DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct pass@1 15.9% → pass@250 56%, beats sing-sample with GPT-4o, 43%).
