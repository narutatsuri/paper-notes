## Paper Notes

Summary: Studies introspection---explaining a models' internal mechanisms from the perspective/hypothesis that language models may be best suited to understand their own internal representations ("Privileged Access Hypothesis"). Train models to be explainers for three types of internal information: internal features (e.g., specific SAE feature values), activation patching outcomes, and input ablation outcomes, and examines what serves as the best explainer model. Finds that similarity of the explainer model to the target model correlates with success; more sharply, best sample efficiency is achieved when explainer = target model. Ablations show that bigger model is surprisingly not necessarily better among others.

## Critique
- When the residual stream dimensions are different between explainer and target, a linear map is learned. But what if we use a much more capable model, e.g., Qwen3.8-27B, or a more expressive mapping that is representation-aligned? At the end of the day the problem (at least for feature descriptions) reduces to learning a decoder for a direction, and there seems to be no good reason why the model itself is the best decoder, at least for the specific test setting where the representation to be interpreted is just inserted alongside the text. What if we try learning a 2-layer NN as the mapping also for the case where explainer = target and other explainer models to see if the same result holds? (To remove the confound of the 2-NN's expressivity taking over, can train it first to "align" the explainer model's representations with the target's. Namely for mapping g(\cdot), train to minimize g(h_{l,t}^{M}(x)) - h_{l,t}^{E}(x) where M is target model and E is explainer model.)

- Here, the target model is frozen, and a copy of the model is trained to be an explainer. But what about using the exact same model? (This seems to potentially create a "chasing" problem, but it feels intuitively that it should converge eventually over training. Or, even, this chasing mechanism where the model gradually changes its internals makes it actually easier to explain?)

- (May be off-topic) How training models to be good self-explainers carries over to more faithful LLM generations seems to make the (strong) assumption that faithful explanation training generalizes: training a model to perfectly self-annotate will make the model universally more faithful in its generations (e.g., thinking trace for reasoning models). This may be true for instruct models but could be a far jump for thinking models; ensuring the thinking trace is more faithful seems to be a separate beast.
